Get them out — and keep them out

Trapping a few mice feels like progress, but if the gaps they used are still open, more will follow. Rodents breed quickly, contaminate food, and gnaw constantly — including on electrical wiring, a known cause of house fires.

Our approach pairs effective removal with thorough exclusion: we find and seal the entry points around your foundation, pipes, and vents so the problem is solved at the root, not just for a week.

Know the Signs

Signs of Mice or Rats

Rodents are most active at night — these clues usually appear first.

Droppings

Small dark, rice-shaped droppings in cupboards, drawers, and along walls.

Gnaw marks

Chewed packaging, wood, plastic, or wiring — rodents gnaw constantly.

Sounds at night

Scratching, scurrying, or squeaking in walls, ceilings, or the attic after dark.

Nests

Shredded paper, fabric, or insulation tucked into hidden, sheltered spaces.

Grease marks

Dark rub marks along baseboards and beams where rodents travel repeatedly.

Chewed food

Gnawed boxes and bags in the pantry, often with droppings nearby.

FAQ

Rodent Questions, Answered

How are mice and rats getting into my home?

A mouse can squeeze through a gap the width of a pencil. They enter through foundation cracks, gaps around pipes and vents, and worn door seals — which is why sealing entry points is essential.

Is entry-point sealing included?

Yes. Sealing the gaps rodents use (exclusion) is the part that keeps them out for good, so it's a core part of our rodent service — not an add-on.

Are rodents a health or fire risk?

Yes. Rodents contaminate food and surfaces and can spread disease, and their constant gnawing on wiring is a recognized cause of house fires. Prompt removal matters.

Do you offer humane removal?

Yes. We focus on safe, discreet removal and on sealing your home so the problem doesn't return, with options suited to your situation.

How long does rodent control take?

Removal often begins on the first visit, with follow-up checks to confirm activity has stopped. Sealing is completed alongside so rodents can't re-enter. See our seasonal guide on fall rodent-proofing.
